## 1. Preferences: verify and change the address (design 4.2, F2)

Files: `prefs.php`, `lib/prefs_email.php` (new), `lib/account_verify.php` (new), `lib/saveuser.php`,
`lib/account_mail.php`.

- **The address leaves the save form.** Preferences' save form no longer has the 1.1.2 `email` field, and
  `saveuser()` never writes `emailaddress`, `emailverified` or `emailvalidation` (`RESURRECTION_SAVEUSER_NEVER`), so
  nothing a page or module puts in `$session['user']` changes the address. A form that still sends `email` has it
  ignored, as any other non-setting field.
- **The "Email address" section** above the preferences form shows the address and, while account mail is on, whether
  it is verified, and a line when a change is waiting for its confirmation.
- **"Verify my address"** (while account mail is on, for an address not yet verified): POST, CSRF and an action token
  bound to the account and the address it was shown for. It mails a verify link (24 hours, once) to the current
  address, within the limits (3 an hour and 6 a day per account, 5 a day per address, the global limit). A new link
  replaces a live one.
- **"Change my address"**: POST, CSRF and an action token bound to the account and its login generation; the current
  password (checked first), then the anchored address check (`resurrection_mail_address_valid()`), "That is already
  your address", and `blockdupeemail` ("That address cannot be used", which names no account). An empty address is
  allowed only while account mail is off and `requireemail` is off.
  - **While account mail is on**, nothing changes until the link mailed to the new address is confirmed. The request
    is counted (3 a day per account) and a new request retires the earlier one. The token is bound to the new address;
    a companion row (purpose `email-from`, never mailed, its hash random) holds the old address, so the confirmation
    can require the account still to have it. No schema change was needed. A verified old address gets a notice of
    the request ("A change of your email address was requested") and, after the confirmation, of the change ("Your
    email address was changed"); neither holds the new address or a link, and neither says who asked.
  - **The confirmation** (`account.php?op=confirm`, the link's grant): the GET page says what Confirm will do; the POST,
    in one transaction, locks the token and the account, checks the companion is live and the account still has the old
    address, checks `blockdupeemail` again, sets `emailaddress` and `emailverified` to the new address on the old
    address and `authversion` conditions (one row or nothing), spends both rows and retires the account's verify links.
    A sign-in, a sign-out or a password change in between raises `authversion` and kills the link.
  - **While account mail is off**, the change takes effect at once, in one transaction, still with the current password
    and the same checks, and leaves the address unverified. This fixes F2 on every host.
- **Dismiss** for the verify prompt posts here too (POST with CSRF, nothing else in the body).

Every new operation calls `resurrection_require_post()` itself, because `prefs.php` checks CSRF only when the body is
not empty: a test found that an empty POST, or a GET, to `op=dismissverify` acted without CSRF before that line was
added, and now asserts 403 for both.

Tests: `MailEmailChangeTest` (6), `MailVerificationTest::testVerifyMailLimitsAndReplacement`,
`MailVerificationTest::testAnyAddressChangeLeavesTheAccountUnverified`,
`test_account_verification_http.py::test_preferences_verify_and_the_prompt`,
`test_account_verification_http.py::test_email_change_needs_password_and_confirmation`.

## 2. Sign-up verification (design 4.1, decision 4)

Files: `create.php`, `login.php`, `account.php`, `lib/accounts.php`, `lib/account_verify.php`,
`lib/account_recovery.php`.

While `requirevalidemail` is effectively on (section 3):

- **The form** marks the address required and says "A link to confirm your address is mailed to it. You can sign in
  once it is confirmed." A missing or invalid address is refused (400). Sign-ups are counted per requester and the
  sixth in an hour gets 429, "Too many accounts were made from here. Try again later.", with no account made.
- **The account** is made with `emailvalidation` `rz-pending`. The page "Check your inbox" ("... Unconfirmed accounts
  are removed after N day(s). Check your spam folder too.") is sent first, and the verify mail goes after it
  (`resurrection_account_send_then()`, moved from `account.php` into `lib/account_recovery.php` so both pages share
  it).
- **Sign-in.** `resurrection_sign_in_check()` (wrapped by `resurrection_authenticate()`, whose callers are unchanged)
  checks the password first. A wrong password gets the usual refusal and nothing else. After the right password, a
  pending account is not signed in: the session id rotates, a 10-minute resend grant for that account is stored, and
  the browser goes (303) to `account.php?op=resend`, "Confirm your email address first", with "Send the link again"
  (POST with CSRF; within the verify limits). Without the grant (another browser, a wrong password, after 10 minutes)
  the page is the fixed expired page.
- **Confirming** (`account.php?op=confirm`, a verify grant): the GET page names the address and the account; the POST
  sets `emailverified` to the address and clears the pending marker, in one transaction on the address and
  `authversion` conditions. "Address confirmed ... The account can sign in now."
- **Cleanup**: a pending account has never signed in, so maintenance removes it under `expiretrashacct` (default 1
  day), with the usual hooks and log line.
- **The user editor's** existing action that clears `emailvalidation` still lets the account sign in.

While account mail is off, a pending account signs in as any other (`resurrection_account_pending_blocked()`); it keeps
its marker, so it is held again if account mail comes back on before it confirms.

Tests: `MailVerificationTest::testPendingSignupCannotSignInWhileAccountMailIsOn`,
`::testPendingSignupCanSignInWhileAccountMailIsOff`, `::testConfirmSetsEmailverifiedAndClearsThePendingMarker`,
`::testAVerifyLinkDiesWithAChangedAddressAStaleAuthversionOrAccountMailOff`, `::testAPendingSignupIsRemovedByTheTrashRule`;
`test_account_verification_http.py::test_signup_verification_and_resend`,

## 3. The lock (design 4.3, decision 5)

Files: `lib/settings.php`, `lib/configuration_descriptors.php`, `lib/typed_editor.php`.

The hard lock of task 7 becomes the conditional rule. `resurrection_locked_settings()` returns
`['requirevalidemail' => '0']` unless `resurrection_requirevalidemail_allowed()` holds (a database connection and
account mail effectively on), and `[]` while it holds; `getsetting()` asks only for the names in
`RESURRECTION_LOCKABLE_SETTINGS`. So the setting reads as on only while account mail is effectively on, and reads as
off again as soon as the switch goes off, or `serverurl` or the sender changes. Game Settings shows it locked, with
"Locked off. Turn on account mail first (Game Settings, Account mail): a sign-up can confirm its address only while the
game can send mail. ...", and refuses a form that sends it (400), while account mail is off; while it is on, it is an
ordinary yes/no setting.

Tests: `MailVerificationTest::testRequireValidEmailReadsOnOnlyWhileAccountMailIsOn` (the layout, the save and the
reason; on with mail on, off again after a `serverurl` change and after a sender change),
`LockedSettingsTest` (unchanged, still passing: off whatever the row holds while account mail is off),
`test_account_verification_http.py::test_requirevalidemail_in_game_settings`,
`test_web_application.py::test_requirevalidemail_is_locked_off_http` (changed, see "Tests changed").

## 5. The verify prompt (the 13c addition)

Files: `lib/account_verify.php`, `lib/pageparts.php`, `lib/prefs_email.php`, `assets/ui/resurrection.css`.

While account mail is on, a signed-in player whose address is not empty and not verified sees one line above the
page's content: "Password recovery by email works only for a verified address, and yours is not verified. Verify it in
Preferences.", the link being the page's own Preferences navigation, with a Dismiss button (POST to
`prefs.php?op=dismissverify` with CSRF). Dismissing stores the address's key in the player's `verifyprompt` preference,
so it stays hidden until the address changes. It shows only on pages that offer Preferences (never in a fight, on
`account.php`, an operator page or a page without a footer), never while account mail is off, and uses no inline script
or style (classes in `resurrection.css`).

Tests: `MailVerificationTest::testTheVerifyPromptShowsOnlyWhileAccountMailIsOnAndTheAddressIsUnverified`,
`::testDismissHidesThePromptForThatAddressOnly`, `test_account_verification_http.py::test_preferences_verify_and_the_prompt`
(shown, dismissed, kept dismissed, shown again after an address change, 403 for a dismiss without CSRF or by GET).
